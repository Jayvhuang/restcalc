Bedtime Calculator

You set the alarm for 7:00, but what time should you actually get into bed? Guessing usually leaves you either staring at the ceiling or jolted awake mid-dream. The Restcalc Bedtime Calculator works backward from your wake time in 90-minute sleep cycles, so you land at a light-sleep boundary instead of deep sleep.

How a sleep cycle works

A night of sleep is built from cycles of about 90 minutes. Each cycle moves through light sleep, deep sleep, and REM (dream) sleep. You feel best when you wake at the edge of a cycle — during light sleep — because your brain is already close to awake. Waking from deep sleep is what causes that heavy, groggy feeling.

So the goal is not "sleep as long as possible." It is "wake at the right moment." That is why counting back in cycles beats picking an arbitrary bedtime.

The math behind your bedtime

Restcalc takes your wake time, subtracts about 15 minutes to fall asleep, then steps back in 90-minute chunks — each chunk is one cycle. Five cycles (7.5 hours) is a common sweet spot for adults.

Example: wake at 7:00. Subtract 15 minutes → you want to be asleep by 6:45. Five cycles back is 7.5 hours → bedtime about 23:15. Six cycles → 22:15. Pick the one that fits your evening, then let the Bedtime Calculator do the arithmetic.

Why 15 minutes to fall asleep

Most people take 10–20 minutes to fall asleep. Restcalc uses 15 as a fair default. If you know you usually take longer, shift your target bedtime a little earlier in your head — or just enter a slightly later wake time and recompute.

How many cycles do you need

Adults generally do well with 4–6 full cycles, or about 7–9 hours in bed. Five cycles (7.5 hours) is the often-cited sweet spot. Consistency matters more than hitting an exact number — a regular schedule trains your body clock better than a perfect but rare bedtime.

Actually hitting that bedtime

  • Start winding down 30–60 minutes before: dim lights, no screens.
  • Keep the same wake time every day, even weekends — it anchors the whole cycle.
  • Avoid late caffeine (see the Caffeine Cutoff Calculator).
  • Run the Bedtime Calculator the night before, not at 11 pm in a panic.
