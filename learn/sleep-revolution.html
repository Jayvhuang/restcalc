The Sleep Revolution: Nick Littlehales' R90 Sleep Method, Explained

A practical, science-backed sleep system used by elite athletes — and how to run it with Restcalc.

《睡眠革命》(The Sleep Revolution) by Nick Littlehales is one of the most influential sleep books of the past decade. It is not a vague "sleep more" manifesto — it is a concrete, method-driven system built around 90-minute sleep cycles. Littlehales is a leading elite-sports sleep coach who has worked with Team Sky, British Cycling, and top football clubs. His core message: stop chasing a fixed "8 hours" and start managing sleep as repeatable 90-minute cycles.

1. The big idea: sleep is a skill, not a passive state

Littlehales reframes sleep as something you actively manage, like training or nutrition. Three pillars run through the whole method:

  • Sleep is trainable. You can improve how you sleep the same way you improve fitness.
  • Quality and cycle alignment beat raw hours. Waking at the right moment matters more than a bigger number on the clock.
  • One fixed anchor organizes everything. That anchor is your wake time, not your bedtime.

2. R90: count in 90-minute cycles, not hours

Sleep arrives in cycles of roughly 90 minutes, moving through light sleep, deep sleep, and REM (dream) sleep. A typical adult night holds 4–6 cycles. The R90 method counts in cycles, not hours:

  • Most adults need about 5 cycles (≈7.5 hours) per night; some do well on 4, others on 6.
  • Instead of "I'll sleep 8 hours," you plan "I'll complete 5 cycles."
  • Missing one cycle occasionally is fine — you recover it across the week (the target is roughly 28–35 cycles per week).

3. Anchor everything to a fixed wake time

Pick a wake time and never vary it — not even on weekends. Everything else is counted back from it:

Target bedtime = wake time − 5 cycles (×90 min) − ~15 min to fall asleep.

This is exactly what Restcalc's Bedtime Calculator does: you enter your wake time, and it counts back in 90-minute cycles plus a 15-minute wind-down. A consistent wake time stabilizes your entire body clock.

4. Work with your chronotype (lark vs owl)

People split into lark (early) and owl (late) types. Forcing an owl to wake at 6 a.m. fights their biology. Practical takeaways:

  • Schedule demanding, high-focus tasks in your personal peak window.
  • Use Restcalc's age group and free-time "sleep window" so the math fits your real life, not an idealized schedule.

5. Build a sleep-friendly bedroom: 16–18°C, dark, quiet, screen-free

  • Temperature: keep the room around 16–18°C (60–65°F). A slight drop in core temperature signals sleep.
  • Darkness: blackout curtains or an eye mask. Evening light — especially blue light from screens — suppresses melatonin.
  • Quiet: earplugs or white noise if needed.
  • No screens in bed: usher phones and laptops out of the bedroom entirely.

6. Choose bedding that keeps your spine neutral

A mattress and pillow should keep your spine in a straight line when you sleep on your side (the recommended position). Go for the largest mattress you can fit to reduce partner disturbance. There is no universal "firmness" — only what keeps you aligned.

7. The 90-minute pre-sleep wind-down

For the 90 minutes before bed: no large meals, no intense exercise, no screens. Do a gentle "transition ritual" instead — tidy up, prep tomorrow, light reading, stretching, or meditation. This tells your brain the day is done.

8. The 15-minute post-wake ramp-up

After waking, don't grab the phone. Get some light (daylight is best) to reset the clock, move a little, and hydrate. Give yourself about 15 minutes to ramp up before screens and stress.

9. Recover lost cycles with strategic naps

If you miss cycles at night, recover during the day: a 20–30 minute power nap or a full 90-minute cycle nap earlier in the day. Naps are a tool, not a sign of failure — just keep them before mid-afternoon so they don't steal from nighttime sleep.

10. How Restcalc turns the method into math

Every R90 idea maps to a Restcalc tool:

  • Bedtime / Wake-up Calculators — count cycles forward and back from your anchor.
  • Sleep Window Helper — fit full cycles into the hours you're actually free.
  • Nap Calculator — plan a recovery nap that lands on a cycle boundary.
  • Caffeine Cutoff Calculator — protect the wind-down by finding your last safe cup.
  • Bedtime Breathing Timer — run the pre-sleep relaxation ritual.
  • Sleep Log & Trend — track your cycles across a week, not just one night.

11. Quick reference

  • Anchor: one fixed wake time, every day.
  • Unit: 90-minute cycles (4–6 per night, ~28–35 per week).
  • Bedroom: 16–18°C, dark, quiet, no screens.
  • Pre-sleep: 90-minute wind-down.
  • Post-wake: 15-minute ramp-up with light.
  • Recover: strategic naps, not guilt.

12. Authoritative sources

  • Littlehales, N. & Credé, R. (2016). Sleep: The Myth of the 8-Hour Sleep and the R90 Method (《睡眠革命》).
  • National Sleep Foundation — sleep-duration recommendations (2022).
  • Sleep Foundation & Cleveland Clinic — blue light, melatonin, and paced breathing.
  • Kleitman & Aserinsky — discovery of REM sleep (1953).
  • Walker, M. (2017). Why We Sleep.

书籍拆解

睡眠革命：尼克·利特尔黑尔斯的 R90 睡眠法全解

一套被顶尖运动员采用的、有科学支撑的睡眠系统——以及如何用 Restcalc 把它跑起来。

《睡眠革命》是尼克·利特尔黑尔斯（Nick Littlehales）写的一本近十年最具影响力的睡眠书。它不是泛泛的"多睡点"口号，而是以 90 分钟睡眠周期为核心的、可操作的方法体系。利特尔黑尔斯是顶尖的竞技体育睡眠教练，曾服务天空车队、英国自行车队和多家顶级足球俱乐部。他的核心主张是：别再执着于固定的"8 小时"，而要把睡眠当成可重复的 90 分钟周期来管理。

1. 核心主张：睡眠是能力，不是被动状态

利特尔黑尔斯把睡眠重新定义为可以主动管理的事，就像训练或饮食。整套方法贯穿三条原则：

  • 睡眠可训练。你能改善睡眠，就像你能提升体能。
  • 质量与周期对齐胜过纯时长。在正确的时刻醒来，比 clock 上更大的数字更重要。
  • 一个固定锚点统领全局。这个锚点是你的起床时间，而非就寝时间。

2. R90：以 90 分钟周期计数，而非小时

睡眠以约 90 分钟为一个周期，在浅睡、深睡与快速眼动（REM）之间循环。成年人一夜通常有 4–6 个周期。R90 方法以周期而非小时来计数：

  • 多数人每晚约需 5 个周期（约 7.5 小时）；有人 4 个就够，有人需要 6 个。
  • 与其说"我要睡 8 小时"，不如规划"我要完成 5 个周期"。
  • 偶尔少一个周期没关系——你可以在一周内补回（目标大约是每周 28–35 个周期）。

3. 一切以固定起床时间为锚

定一个起床时间，并且绝不改动——周末也一样。其余都从它倒推：

目标就寝时间 = 起床时间 − 5 个周期（×90 分钟）− 约 15 分钟入睡时间。

这正是 Restcalc 就寝计算器的逻辑：你输入起床时间，它按 90 分钟周期倒推，并预留约 15 分钟放松。固定的起床时间能稳定你整个生物钟。

4. 顺应你的睡眠类型（早起型 vs 晚睡型）

人分为早起型（云雀）与晚睡型（猫头鹰）。强迫猫头鹰早上 6 点起床，是和自己的生理对着干。实用做法：

  • 把高难度、需专注的任务安排在个人精力高峰。
  • 用 Restcalc 的年龄段与空闲"睡眠窗口"，让计算贴合你的真实生活，而非理想日程。

5. 打造助眠卧室：16–18°C、黑暗、安静、无屏幕

  • 温度：保持约 16–18°C（60–65°F）。核心体温小幅下降会发出"该睡了"的信号。
  • 黑暗：遮光窗帘或眼罩。傍晚的光线——尤其是屏幕蓝光——会抑制褪黑素。
  • 安静：必要时用耳塞或白噪音。
  • 床上不放屏幕：把手机和笔记本彻底请出卧室。

6. 选择让脊柱中立的寝具

床垫与枕头应在你侧卧（推荐睡姿）时让脊柱保持一条直线。床越大越好，以减少伴侣间的干扰。没有放之四海皆准的"硬度"——只有让你保持对齐的那一种。

7. 睡前 90 分钟放松程序

睡前 90 分钟：不进正餐、不做剧烈运动、不看屏幕。改做温和的"过渡仪式"——整理、准备明日、轻松阅读、拉伸或冥想。这告诉大脑：今天结束了。

8. 醒后 15 分钟缓启程序

醒来后别急着抓手机。接触光线（自然光最佳）以重置生物钟，稍微活动、补水。给自己约 15 分钟缓缓启动，再碰屏幕与压力。

9. 用策略性小睡补回缺失的周期

若夜间周期不足，可在白天补回：20–30 分钟强力小睡或一整个 90 分钟周期小睡。小睡是工具，不是失败的标志——只要放在午后之前，就不会偷走夜间睡眠。

10. Restcalc 如何把方法变成计算

R90 的每个理念都对应一个 Restcalc 工具：

  • 就寝 / 起床计算器——从你的锚点向前、向后数周期。
  • 睡眠窗口助手——把完整周期塞进你真正空闲的时段。
  • 小睡计算器——安排落在周期边界上的补救小睡。
  • 咖啡因截止计算器——找到最后一杯安全咖啡，守护放松窗口。
  • 睡前放松呼吸计时器——执行睡前放松仪式。
  • 睡眠记录与趋势——跟踪你一周的周期，而非孤零零的一晚。

11. 要点速览

  • 锚点：每天固定的起床时间。
  • 单位：90 分钟周期（每晚 4–6 个，每周约 28–35 个）。
  • 卧室：16–18°C、黑暗、安静、无屏幕。
  • 睡前：90 分钟放松。
  • 醒后：15 分钟缓启 + 光线。
  • 补救：策略性小睡，而非自责。
