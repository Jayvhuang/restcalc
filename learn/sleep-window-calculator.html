Sleep Window Helper

Your sleep hours are not fixed — classes, shifts, or a crying kid decide when you can be in bed. The Restcalc Sleep Window Helper takes the time range you are free to sleep and finds the bedtimes that complete full 90-minute cycles and stay inside that window.

The problem with an irregular schedule

When bedtime moves around, your body clock never settles. You may still get hours, but the cycles land at different phases, so quality drops even when total time looks fine.

The helper narrows the guesswork: given a window, it shows which bedtimes actually work.

How it finds the bedtimes

You enter the range you are free (say 22:00–02:00). The tool counts forward in 90-minute cycles from a wake target, then lists the bedtimes that both complete full cycles and stay inside your window.

Use the Sleep Window Helper to see the options for your night.

Why staying inside the window matters

Consistency of timing trains the circadian rhythm more than total hours do. A regular window — even if short — beats a long but random schedule.

Pick the bedtime in the window you can hit most nights, not the one that looks best on paper once.

Who this is for

Shift workers, students with varying schedules, parents, and travelers — anyone whose free hours shift day to day. It turns "whenever I crash" into a plan.

Tips for an irregular life

  • Aim for the same wake time even when the window moves.
  • Inside the window, protect the last 30 minutes as wind-down.
  • Use the Sleep Log to see whether the window actually holds.
  • If the window is very short, protect sleep debt with a strategic nap.
