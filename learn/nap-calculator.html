Nap Calculator

A good nap refreshes; a bad one leaves you worse. The Restcalc Nap Calculator takes your current time and shows the best window — either a short power nap or a full 90-minute cycle — so you wake alert instead of groggy.

Two nap lengths that actually work

A 10–20 minute power nap stays in light sleep, so you wake alert with no grogginess. A full 90-minute nap completes a cycle, including some REM, and also leaves you refreshed.

Both avoid the danger zone in between — the part that backfires.

Why 30–60 minutes is the trap

A nap of about 30–60 minutes often drops you into deep sleep. Waking from there causes sleep inertia — that heavy, disoriented feeling. That is why a "quick 45-minute nap" can leave you worse than no nap.

If you only have an hour, a 20-minute nap is usually the safer bet. The Nap Calculator picks the length that fits your clock.

Timing matters as much as length

Nap early in the afternoon. A late nap steals sleep pressure from the coming night and makes bedtime harder. The calculator factors your current time so the suggestion fits the day you are in.

As a rule of thumb, finish napping by early-to-mid afternoon.

Who benefits most

Naps help shift workers, people carrying a sleep debt, or anyone with a post-lunch dip. They are a top-up, not a substitute for a full night of sleep.

Tips for a clean nap

  • Set an alarm so you don't drift into deep sleep.
  • Keep the room dark and cool.
  • Avoid caffeine right before a nap.
  • If naps hurt your nighttime sleep, move them earlier or drop them.
