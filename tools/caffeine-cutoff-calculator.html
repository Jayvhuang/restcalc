How the caffeine cutoff calculator works

A caffeine cutoff calculator takes the time you plan to sleep and counts backward by a buffer that depends on how sensitive you are. Most adults clear about half the caffeine in their system every 5 hours — a half-life of roughly 3–7 hours — so a late cup can still be active at bedtime. The calculator turns "when should I stop?" into one clock time.

If you have ever wondered "why does my 3 p.m. coffee keep me up?", the answer is pharmacokinetics, not willpower. Caffeine blocks adenosine — the chemical that builds your sleep pressure — so even after it wears off, the accumulated adenosine hits at once. The cutoff exists to keep that residue below the level that fragments sleep.

Why does caffeine hurt sleep even if you feel fine?

EEG sleep studies show caffeine reduces deep sleep and total sleep time even in people who insist they are "unaffected." Tolerance masks how caffeine feels but does not protect your sleep architecture. That is why a fixed cutoff — not "however much I can handle" — is the reliable rule. Restcalc's 6 / 8 / 10–12 hour tiers follow the same guidance sleep researchers use, such as the National Sleep Foundation and studies in the Journal of Clinical Sleep Medicine (2013).

A few quick tips

  • Set a daily caffeine curfew and switch to decaf or herbal tea after it.
  • Remember hidden sources: tea, cola, energy drinks, pre-workout, and dark chocolate.
  • If you need an afternoon lift, try a short walk or a 10–20 minute nap instead.
